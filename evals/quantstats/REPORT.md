## Findings

**QS-1 (High, correctness) - `kelly_criterion` returns the growth-optimal fraction multiplied by the average loss, so it understates leverage by roughly 100x on daily returns.** `stats.py:2553-2575`. The code is the textbook two-outcome form `f = (b*p - q)/b` with `b = payoff_ratio = avg_win/|avg_loss|`. That form assumes a losing bet forfeits 100% of the stake. Applied to a return series whose losing periods are a fraction of a percent, it returns the exact answer scaled by the loss magnitude. Reproduced in closed form: on a two-outcome series with `p=0.60`, win `+2%`, loss `-1%`, the growth-optimal fraction is `39.998500` and HEAD returns `0.399985`, a ratio of exactly `1/0.01 = 100`; with `p=0.55` and `+/-1%`, `9.964000` against `0.099640`; with `p=0.60`, `+20%/-10%`, `3.999850` against `0.399985`, ratio exactly `1/0.10 = 10`. It is also invariant to the scale of the returns, which no capital-allocation recommendation can be: scaling one 10-year daily series by 1.0, 0.5 and 0.1 leaves HEAD at `0.03554685` in all three cases while the growth-optimal fraction moves `2.8618 / 5.7236 / 28.6182`. Issue [#535](https://github.com/ranaroussi/quantstats/issues/535) asks for the continuous Kelly; the sharper statement is that even in the discrete case the formula is being fed the wrong units. Fixed by solving the growth-optimal condition directly - the `f` for which `E[r/(1+f*r)] = 0`, via `scipy.optimize.brentq` on the interval the objective is concave over. That is exact for two-outcome series (it reproduces all three closed forms above to 1e-6) and exact for continuous ones, where a discrete form rebuilt from `avg_win`/`avg_loss` would still have been 57% to 100% too high. **This changes observable output; the adoption decision is routed under Proposed below.**

**QS-2 (High, correctness) - `rar()` discards the report's periods-per-year and always annualizes at 252, two rows below a CAGR computed at the report's own frequency.** `stats.py:1561-1585` (`return cagr(returns) / exposure(returns)`, no `periods` forwarded) and `reports.py:1444` (`metrics["Risk-Adjusted Return %"] = _get_stats().rar(df, rf) * pct`). Reproduced: on 11 years of monthly returns with `periods_per_year=12` and full exposure, `cagr(m, 0.0, True, 12) = 0.08975000448300419` while `rar(m) = 5.079452491926482`. The two must be equal when exposure is 1.0; they differ by **56.60x**. In the tearsheet that is CAGR `8.98%` and Risk-Adjusted Return `507.95%` in the same table for the same strategy. Fixed by giving `rar` a `periods` argument, forwarding it to `cagr`, and passing `win_year` at the report call site. The daily default path is unchanged.

**QS-3 (High, correctness) - the risk-free rate is routed by caller function name, so `cagr` and `gain_to_pain_ratio` silently discard it, and a negative rate is discarded everywhere.** `utils.py:610` (`function = inspect.stack()[1][3]`), `utils.py:632-641` (the `unnecessary_function_calls` exclusion list and `if rf > 0:`). Reproduced: `cagr(r, rf=0.00) == cagr(r, rf=0.05) == cagr(r, rf=0.50) == 0.2163540607973844` to every digit, while `sharpe` on the same series moves `0.6258357361` to `0.3224652536`. `gain_to_pain_ratio(r, rf=0.00) == gain_to_pain_ratio(r, rf=0.05) == 0.23518822033805786`. Separately, `if rf > 0` drops negative rates: `sharpe(r, rf=0) == sharpe(r, rf=-0.005) == sharpe(r, rf=-0.02) == 0.62583574`, so a decade of EUR and JPY policy rates would have been silently ignored by every metric in the module. `cagr`'s own docstring at `stats.py:1514` says "Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) **of excess returns**" and documents `rf`. The author's intent is proved inside `reports.py` itself: the CAGR row is `cagr(df, rf, ...)` at line 1314 while `3Y`, `5Y`, `10Y` and `All-time (ann.)` are all `cagr(df[...], 0.0, ...)` at lines 1550-1563. At HEAD all five of those calls return the same gross number, so CAGR `10.71%`, Expected Yearly `10.71%` and All-time (ann.) `10.71%` are one number pretending to be three; after the fix the CAGR row reads `5.43%` and All-time (ann.) stays `10.71%`, which is the distinction the report was written to draw. Fixed by deleting the `inspect.stack()` dispatch in favour of an explicit `apply_rf` parameter, changing `if rf > 0` to `if rf != 0`, and settling each of the four sites that relied on the name lookup (enumerated below).

**QS-4 (High, correctness) - `aggregate_returns` silently ignores the documented period codes `'M'`, `'Q'` and `'Y'`, and raises on `'W'`.** `utils.py:443-507`. Twelve public functions in `stats.py` carry the docstring line `aggregate (str): Aggregation period ('D', 'W', 'M', 'Q', 'Y')` - `expected_return`, `geometric_mean`, `ghpr`, `best`, `worst`, `consecutive_wins`, `consecutive_losses`, `win_rate`, `avg_return`, `avg_win`, `avg_loss`, `compare` (`stats.py` lines 213, 247, 269, 338, 371, 404, 440, 523, 586, 623, 660, 2800). Of the five documented codes, three fall through every branch and return the unaggregated series, and one raises. Reproduced on 10 years of daily returns: `best(r, aggregate='M') == best(r, aggregate='Q') == best(r, aggregate='Y') == best(r, aggregate=None) == 0.035997`, which is the best **day**, while `best(r, aggregate='ME') = 0.120335`, `'QE' = 0.183417`, `'YE' = 0.178329`. `aggregate_returns(r, 'W')` raises `AttributeError: 'DatetimeIndex' object has no attribute 'week'` at `utils.py:486` and `:490`; `DatetimeIndex.week` was removed in pandas 2.0 and `requirements.txt` pins `pandas>=2.0.0`, so every weekly path in the library is dead. Fixed at the boundary rather than at the twelve call sites: a `_normalize_period` alias map turns pandas offset aliases into the calendar codes the grouping branches actually implement, `.week` becomes `.isocalendar().week`, and an unrecognised code now raises `ValueError` instead of silently returning daily data. All twelve documented sites are covered by that one boundary change; none was patched individually.

**QS-5 (Medium, correctness) - `treynor_ratio`'s documented `periods` argument has no effect on its result, and the numerator is the whole-sample cumulative return.** `stats.py:1355-1391`. `periods` is forwarded to `greeks()` only to annualize alpha, which `treynor_ratio` then discards by taking `.get("beta")`; beta is periods-invariant. Reproduced: `treynor_ratio(r, b, periods=252) == treynor_ratio(r, b, periods=12) == treynor_ratio(r, b, periods=1) == -52.05502714897246`, while the docstring at `stats.py:1366` documents `periods (float): Periods per year for annualization (default: 252.0)`. The numerator, `comp(returns) - rf` at `stats.py:1391`, is the cumulative return over the entire backtest (`1.0037` on this series, i.e. 100.4% over 10 years) netted against an annual `rf`, so the ratio grows with the length of the sample and is not comparable across horizons. Fixed by using `cagr(returns, rf, periods=int(periods))` as the numerator, which annualizes it, nets `rf` at the same frequency, and makes the documented argument do what it says.

**QS-6 (Medium, correctness) - `probabilistic_ratio(annualize=True)` multiplies a probability by a hardcoded `sqrt(252)` and ignores `periods`.** `stats.py:1252-1253`, `return psr * (252**0.5)`. Reproduced: `probabilistic_sharpe_ratio(r, 0.0, 12, True) == probabilistic_sharpe_ratio(r, 0.0, 252, True) == 15.852114`, from a base probability of `0.998589`, against a docstring at `stats.py:1280` promising "Probabilistic Sharpe ratio (0-1 scale)". Two problems in one expression: a cumulative distribution value has no annualization, and the 252 is hardcoded so the `periods` argument is inert on this path. `reports.py:1322` always passes `annualize=False`, so the shipped tearsheet is unaffected; a direct API caller is not. Fixed by returning the probability unscaled and warning that the flag has no meaning on a probability.

**QS-7 (Medium, correctness) - `rolling_greeks` alpha is not a rolling statistic and embeds the full-sample mean, future data included, at every point.** `stats.py:2776`, `alpha = df["returns"].mean() - beta * df["benchmark"].mean()`. Both means are whole-series scalars, so the only thing that varies across the output is beta. Reproduced as an exact identity: `alpha_t + beta_t * benchmark.mean() == returns.mean()` for every `t` in the series (`np.allclose` True across the full 10 years). The first point of a rolling alpha therefore already knows the last day's return. It is also not annualized while `greeks()` annualizes alpha by `* periods` at `stats.py:2719`, so the two functions differ by 252x on the same inputs (`0.000626` against `0.149614`). Fixed by taking rolling means over the same window as the rolling beta.

**QS-8 (Medium, correctness) - `recovery_factor` divides an arithmetically-summed numerator by a compounded denominator.** `stats.py:2357`, `total_returns = returns.sum() - rf`, against `max_drawdown` at `stats.py:2451`, which converts returns to a compounded equity curve before measuring the drawdown. Reproduced: on one 10-year daily series `comp(r) = 2.9678018732` and `r.sum() = 1.5051086635`, a **97.2% gap** for the same "total return"; `recovery_factor` returns `7.5625978202` where the compounded numerator gives `14.9120741387`. Within the same module `ulcer_performance_index` (`stats.py:1737`) and `treynor_ratio` (`stats.py:1391`) use `comp()` for the identical concept. Fixed by compounding the numerator, with a `compounded=False` argument preserving the old convention, and by threading the report's existing `compounded` switch through at `reports.py:1611`.

**QS-9 (Low, correctness) - the 3Y and 5Y annualized-return windows use one offset convention and the 10Y window uses another.** `reports.py:1548` `relativedelta(months=35)`, `:1553` `relativedelta(months=59)`, `:1558` `relativedelta(years=10)`. Reproduced by parsing the offsets out of `inspect.getsource(qs.reports.metrics)`: the residual against each label is `-1`, `-1` and `0` months. On 15 years of daily data the realised calendar spans are `2.919y`, `4.917y` and `9.996y` against labels of 3, 5 and 10 years, and the 10Y CAGR moves from `0.082852` to `0.081858` once the window is made consistent. This is issue [#516](https://github.com/ranaroussi/quantstats/issues/516), and it reproduces exactly as filed. Fixed by changing the 10Y line to `relativedelta(months=119)`, which matches the `n*12-1` convention the other two already follow and leaves 3Y and 5Y untouched.

**QS-10 (High, correctness) - `information_ratio` is not annualized while `sharpe`, in the same module and the same tearsheet block, is.** `stats.py:2635-2673`. Reproduced as an exact identity: against a zero benchmark the information ratio must equal the Sharpe ratio, and at HEAD `information_ratio(r, zeros) = 0.047640` while `sharpe(r, rf=0) = 0.756265`; the ratio is exactly `sqrt(252) = 15.874508`. The docstring is not contradicted - it says "active return divided by the tracking error", which is literally what the code computes. The defect is that `reports.py:1410` prints that per-period number in the row directly below Sharpe, Sortino and Volatility (ann.), all of which are annualized, with no label saying otherwise. A strategy with a genuine annualized information ratio of 1.0 displays 0.06. On the tearsheet exhibit above it moves from `0.01` to `0.17`. Fixed by adding `periods=252, annualize=True` to match `sharpe`'s signature exactly, and passing `win_year` at both report call sites. **This changes observable output; the adoption decision is routed under Proposed below.**

**QS-11 (Low, documentation) - `risk_return_ratio`'s docstring calls it the Sharpe ratio.** `stats.py:2377`, "This function calculates the Sharpe ratio without factoring in the risk-free rate". It is the Sharpe ratio divided by `sqrt(periods)`: on one series `risk_return_ratio(r) = 0.05959049` against `sharpe(r, 0.0, 252) = 0.94596975`, again exactly `sqrt(252)` apart, and both appear in the tearsheet. Fixed in the docstring only. The function's behaviour is left alone deliberately: unlike `information_ratio` it takes no `periods` argument and makes no annualization claim, so changing its output would be seizing a convention rather than fixing an error.

## Leads that did not reproduce, or that are defensible conventions rather than defects

Each is a passing check in `repro.py` in both directions, so the claim is checkable against upstream HEAD as well as against the patch.

- **N-1, issue [#514](https://github.com/ranaroussi/quantstats/issues/514), "information_ratio uses arithmetic mean instead of geometric".** The gap the reporter describes is real and measurable: on 10 years of daily data the arithmetic active return is `0.00007233` against a geometric `0.00006219`, a **16.30%** relative difference. **Not filed as a defect.** The arithmetic difference `returns - benchmark` is the standard Grinold-Kahn active-return convention, it is what mainstream analytics platforms report, and it is exactly what the function's docstring describes. A defensible-but-different convention is not a defect. The real defect in this function is the missing annualization (QS-10), which the issue does not mention.
- **N-2, issue [#518](https://github.com/ranaroussi/quantstats/issues/518), "EOY returns vs benchmark wrong over 10y+ ranges".** **Not reproduced.** Rendered `qs.plots.yearly_returns` at spans of 8.5, 9.5, 10.5 and 12.5 years and read the axis back programmatically: at every span the x tick labels and both bar containers carry exactly one entry per calendar year, in order, with zero mismatches. The hypothesis in the issue thread, that `_plt.xticks(np.arange(len(years)), ...)` at `_plotting/core.py:275` sets positions pandas did not use, does not hold: pandas places bar categories at `0..n-1`, which is precisely what `arange` supplies. The sparsification branch at `core.py:273` computes `mod = int(len(years)/10)`, which is 1 for 11 to 19 years, so no label is suppressed in the range the reporter describes. At 21 years `mod` becomes 2 and every second label is deliberately blanked, still correctly aligned. Whatever the reporter saw, it is not reproducible from a return series alone on this stack.
- **N-3, issue [#493](https://github.com/ranaroussi/quantstats/issues/493), "trade-analysis metrics are wrong (computed from returns)".** **Not filed as a defect.** The arithmetic is exact for what it computes: `payoff_ratio` is `avg_win/|avg_loss|` of the return series to 1e-12, `profit_factor` is `sum(wins)/|sum(losses)|` to 1e-12. These are period statistics computed correctly as period statistics, and the maintainer's own reply on the issue (2025-12-29) states that this is by design for a returns-analysis library. Whether to rename or remove them is a scope decision for the owner, not an arithmetic error. The single metric on the reporter's list that **is** wrong independently of the trade-versus-returns question is Kelly, filed as QS-1.
- **N-4, `cagr`'s `years = len(returns)/periods` denominator.** **Not filed as a defect.** It is self-documented at `stats.py:1545-1547` ("consistent with how Sharpe, Sortino, and other metrics handle annualization in quantstats"), and that claim is accurate. Verified: on 5 calendar years of 365-observations-per-year data, `cagr(r, 0.0, True, 365) = 0.07572268515731828` against the exact closed form `1.0002**365 - 1 = 0.07572268515731784`, agreeing to 1e-15. The same series at the 252 default returns `0.05168638912134371`, which is the caller's `periods` argument doing exactly what it says it does.
- **N-5, `sortino` divides the downside sum of squares by N while `sharpe` uses ddof=1.** **Not filed as a defect.** Dividing by the full N is the convention in the Red Rock Capital paper the `sortino` docstring cites at `stats.py:1015-1016`, and ddof=1 is correct for a sample standard deviation. Verified as an exact identity to 1e-12. Two different conventions for two different statistics is not an inconsistency.
- **N-6, `greeks` annualizes alpha linearly while `cagr` annualizes geometrically.** **Not filed as a defect.** Linear annualization of Jensen's alpha is standard. Verified as an exact identity to 1e-9.

## Routed to the owner under Proposed, not seized

Three of the fixes change numbers that appear in published tearsheets. The patch implements what this audit believes is right; the adoption decision is the maintainer's.

1. **QS-1, `kelly_criterion` output scale.** Kelly Criterion % moves from `8.25%` to `403.74%` on the exhibit above. The new number is the correct growth-optimal fraction, and full Kelly on a gross-Sharpe-0.7 strategy genuinely is several times levered; the old number looked plausible precisely because it was scaled by the average daily loss, which is why it went unnoticed. If the maintainer prefers a bounded display, the honest options are to report `min(f, 1.0)` under a clearer label, to report half-Kelly, or to keep the raw fraction and document the units. What is not defensible is the current value, which is invariant to the size of the losses.
2. **QS-10, the `information_ratio` default.** Setting `annualize=True` by default changes every existing caller's number by `sqrt(periods)`. The alternative is to default `annualize=False` and change only the report call site. This audit chose the annualized default because an information ratio is annualized by universal convention and the per-period value has no readership, but callers with pinned expectations will notice.
3. **QS-3, `cagr` and `rf`.** The fix makes `cagr` honour `rf`, following its own docstring ("of excess returns"). The equally defensible alternative is to delete `rf` from `cagr`'s signature and document the row as gross. Either resolves the defect. Silently accepting a parameter and ignoring it does not.

Two further conventions were deliberately **not** seized. `ulcer_performance_index` (`stats.py:1706`) and `serenity_index` (`stats.py:1757`) both build their numerator from the whole-sample return rather than an annualized one, and they disagree with each other on whether that return is compounded (`comp()`) or summed (`.sum()`). Neither takes a `periods` argument and neither docstring claims annualization, so there is no docstring contradiction to point at, only a convention choice. They are recorded in the class table and left to the owner.

## Disclosed limitation

`serenity_index` cites the KeyQuant "Adaptive Portfolio Theory" whitepaper at `stats.py:1778-1779`. This audit was conducted fully offline and did not retrieve that paper, so whether its definition specifies an arithmetic or a compounded numerator was not verified. That is why `serenity_index` was left unchanged rather than aligned with `recovery_factor` under QS-8, even though the two disagree on the same quantity: changing a formula whose authority could not be read would have been a guess.

Two further limitations. First, `repro.py` uses `numpy.random.default_rng`, whose PCG64 stream is stable across platforms, but the recorded HEAD constants were observed on pandas 3.0.5 / numpy 2.5.1 / scipy 1.18.0, and the checks use relative tolerances rather than bit equality. Second, the audit is scoped to `stats.py`, `utils.py` and the metrics path of `reports.py`; `_plotting/`, `_montecarlo.py` and the HTML template were examined only where a lead led into them (N-2), and are not claimed to be clean.

## Method notes

The audit ran the five referenced leads to ground first, then audited annualization and compounding as a class rather than as instances, per the class rule: 28 functions enumerated, each probed by measuring its response to a 4x change in `periods` rather than by reading the source, and every `_prepare_returns` call site that depended on the caller-name dispatch enumerated and settled in the table above. Three findings shared the root cause "a ratio that is not annualized while its neighbours in the same tearsheet row block are" (QS-5, QS-10, QS-11); rather than patch a fourth instance, `ulcer_performance_index` and `serenity_index` were routed under Proposed with the enumeration recorded.
