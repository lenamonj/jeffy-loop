# Jeffy eval: PyPortfolio/PyPortfolioOpt

**Target**: [PyPortfolio/PyPortfolioOpt](https://github.com/PyPortfolio/PyPortfolioOpt) (5,905 stars, verified via `gh api repos/PyPortfolio/PyPortfolioOpt --jq '.stargazers_count'` on 2026-07-29) at upstream HEAD `a6638d2e06dae6f444fd022cfd4b3c528902a85b` (2026-07-07), Python 3.13.8 / numpy 2.5.1 / scipy 1.18.0 / cvxpy 1.9.2 / pandas 3.0.5, in a local clone. Nothing was pushed upstream. This is the library people use to turn a price history into position sizes: its outputs are weights that get traded, so a silently wrong weight outranks any crash.

**This is a full `/jeffy` loop run that reached machine-checked convergence.** Six budgeted runs, each in a fresh session, 58 iterations total: 36 task iterations, 16 full audits, 6 wrapups, plus 4 journal rotations. Converged at `b12a86ab911449e0049969c155315c849591a03a` on 2026-07-29 with every condition of the closing rule independently re-verified for this receipt: empty ledger, all 26 surface-inventory rows swept, only loop state changed past the Converged commit, the suite green at **356 passed, 11 skipped, exit 0** from a fresh re-run, and an evaluator PASS.

**The baseline was red.** `pytest ./tests -q` at HEAD: **5 failed, 279 passed, 33 skipped**, every failure sharing one root cause - and the project's own CI `Test` workflow was red at exactly this commit with the same five failures. Fifty-eight iterations later the suite is **356 passed, 11 skipped, 0 failed**, where every remaining skip is a `skipif` naming an absent optional dependency. The diff against upstream is 16 product files, **+846/-129**, plus 13 test files, **+1,271/-50**. The skip count fell early because the loop completed the dev environment (matplotlib, plotly) in iteration 1; four further tests disabled by an unexplained `@pytest.mark.skip` citing a years-old bug report were diagnosed as three distinct causes and repaired rather than re-hidden.

## What the loop found: 37 findings filed, 36 closed, 1 declined with its reason on record

Five Highs, every one a wrong number or a dead feature rather than a crash:

- **PR-01** - `HRPOpt.optimize()` validated `linkage_method` against the private scipy attribute `sch._LINKAGE_METHODS`, which scipy 1.18 removed. The entire Hierarchical Risk Parity surface and `plot_dendrogram` were unreachable on a current install; this is what had upstream's own CI red. Fixed by deleting the pre-check and letting scipy's public `linkage` enforce its own documented `ValueError`.
- **PR-07** - `BlackLittermanModel.bl_weights()` divided the raw solution by its own signed sum, **inverting every position** whenever the implied portfolio was net short.
- **PR-09** - `min_cov_determinant` returned the raw `fast_mcd` covariance without the consistency correction, **understating variance by more than half** at the normal distribution.
- **PR-24** - `EfficientCVaR.portfolio_performance` and `EfficientCDaR.portfolio_performance` reported a risk read off the solver's auxiliary variables rather than the risk of the weights they returned, **overstating CVaR by up to 50.7 percent** on efficient_return calls. A solved value is trustworthy only where the solve pinned it; both now compute risk from the returned weights.
- **PR-31** - all four tail-risk constructors document `expected_returns=None` as accepted; only `EfficientFrontier` honoured it. Closed as one class fix across five sites.

Twelve defect classes were settled with enumerating checks, three of them forced by the three-strike rule into structural closes at a single boundary rather than a fourth patch: every cvxpy error converted to the library's own `OptimizationError` with an exhaustiveness test that fails if cvxpy adds an error class; every reported feasibility limit derived from the caller's actual constraint set; and a memoised-limit class (PR-21/22/23, seeded by an evaluator rejection) closed with a cache key proven complete against cvxpy's two mutation channels. The loop also shipped the `scipy<2.0.0` upper bound that keeps the PR-01 class from recurring, and left one genuine owner decision - re-enabling `set_weights` on the tail-risk classes, whose original reason to exist PR-24 removed - under Proposed rather than seizing it.

## The gate that would not be bluffed

The adversarial evaluator ran **seven times across the conversion: five REJECTs before two PASSes**, and every rejection produced filed findings rather than friction - one REJECT's evidence became the memoised-limit class, and a second REJECT in the same run ended it as a hard blocker. The sharpest moment is run 5: the evaluator returned **PASS and the run refused to converge anyway**, because one of the evaluator's own Low observations was a defect the run had introduced in a docstring it wrote. Its final entry reads: *"any source change made now would be the only change in the run that no adversarial reviewer had seen, and convergence declared over it would hollow out the gate that just did its job. The gate is worth more than the convergence line."* The finding was filed as PR-35 and closed by the next run under a full evaluator budget. The same evaluator's parting observation - CLA crashing on all-equal expected returns - became run 6's PR-36.

Convergence itself was declared twice. The first declaration was **rejected by the Stop hook**: its own re-run of the Verify command exited 127, because the Verify line in PLAN.md carried markdown backticks that `bash -c` treats as command substitution. The run diagnosed the mechanism, fixed the line, proved the hook's exact pipeline green, and re-declared on a byte-identical code tree. The machine gate rejected its own victory lap until the evidence executed.

The final evaluator PASS re-ran the suite directly, proved all three of the closing run's fixes red against their pre-fix sources restored from git archive, re-derived a 198-float differential against the pre-run tree at zero difference, and re-ran the settled classes' enumerating checks by hand.

## Compared with the manual audit - in both directions, because neither wins

An earlier version of this receipt was a single-pass manual audit of the same commit, with a 13-check offline repro. That repro, run today against the loop's converged tree, scores **8 of 13** - and each side of that number is instructive.

**Both instruments found, independently, with no hints passed**: the HRP dead-feature High (identical fix, deleting the private-attribute pre-check); the equal-expected-returns degeneracy (the loop's constructor refusal names `EfficientFrontier` as the alternative, the same shape the audit chose, and the audit's 300-problem probe now scores 300 clear errors, 0 wrong portfolios); NaN rejection at the CLA boundary; and the escaping cvxpy `SolverError` (the loop's version is class-complete where the audit's patched one tuple).

**The audit found 3 defects the loop did not, and they are still present at the converged tree.** CLA builds turning points from a stale weight vector, so **50 of 300 synthetic problems produce an off-budget turning point and 22 of 300 return a `max_sharpe` portfolio up to 2.0156 percent short of the true optimum, silently** - the loop's CLA sweep cross-checked against `EfficientFrontier` on the shipped fixture and got agreement to 1e-4, because the fixture is not among the roughly 7 percent of problems that come back silently short. A singular PSD covariance still crashes CLA with a raw `numpy.linalg.LinAlgError` naming nothing. And the shrinkage estimators still `nan_to_num` missing returns into zeros with no warning, understating BABA's variance by **86.7 percent** on the project's own shipped fixture and putting 43.2 percent of a min-volatility portfolio into it.

The pattern in those three is one pattern: they are **distributional defects**. The audit ran randomized differential campaigns - 300 synthetic problems against an independent solver as oracle - where the loop's sweep contract demands known answers and live parameters on concrete cases. A known-answer probe on a fixture that happens to sit in the defect-free majority certifies the row honestly and misses the tail. That is the current boundary between the loop and a randomized-oracle audit, stated here rather than discovered by a critic; the three findings remain published, reproducible claims against upstream regardless of which instrument found them. (One repro check fails for the opposite reason: its demonstration that the naive `_infnone`-only fix would be worse than the crash can no longer run, because the loop's constructor refuses the degenerate input before the naive fix could matter.)

**The loop filed 32 findings wholly absent from the audit**, four of them High - the sign-inverting `bl_weights`, the uncorrected `min_cov_determinant`, the misreported CVaR/CDaR risk, and the broken `expected_returns=None` contract - plus the misleading-limit classes, the memoised-limit class, the dead `points` parameter on CLA's frontier, the skipped-test diagnosis, and 77 net new passing tests. The audit read the degenerate-input surface deeply and never scored the reporting paths; the loop swept all 26 rows and never randomized. Neither instrument subsumes the other, which is the finding.

**Convergence is therefore a claim about a contract, not about perfection**: under Jeffy's severity rubric, envelope, and sweep contract as they stood, a full fresh-evidence audit found nothing High or Medium left on a fully swept 26-row surface, and an adversarial evaluator countersigned. Three known distributional defects stand outside that contract's reach, named above.
